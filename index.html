Short Bio

I was born and grew up in Shanghai, China. I received the B.S. degree in Industrial Engineering and Management from Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU), Jun 2014, with a minor in Economics. I spent a wonderful Summer 2012 at the Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge, UK, as an exchange student. I also had internship experience in the manufacturing industry and in a public policy consulting firm.

I was fortunate to receive the Ph.D. degree from the Department of Industrial Systems Engineering and Management, National University of Singapore (NUS), Jun 2019, supervised by Prof. William B. Haskell (now at the Supply Chain and Operations Management Department, Mitchell E. Daniels, Jr. School of Business, Purdue University) and Prof. Tang Loon Ching. During my doctoral study, I also had the opportunity to collaborate with Prof. Huifu Xu, and participated in the E2S2-CREATE project under the Campus for Research Excellence and Technological Enterprise, Singapore.

Prior to the current post, recruited by the HKU-100 Global Recruitment Exercise, I held prestigious joint postdoc positions at the School of Data Science, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, China, and the Department of Decision Sciences, HEC Montréal, Québec, Canada (also affiliated with the Shenzhen Research Institute of Big Data (SRIBD) and the Group for Research in Decision Analysis (GERAD)), from Sep 2019 to Sep 2021, working with Prof. Erick Delage and Prof. Zizhuo Wang.

Selected Works

  1. "Preference robust optimization for choice functions on the space of CDFs" (with William B. Haskell and Huifu Xu). Extends preference robust optimization to choice models by constructing a distributionally robust decision framework over the full space of CDFs, with the ambiguity set calibrated from limited preference information. The key contribution is a tractable characterization of the worst-case evaluation, reducing the robust problem to a computable form. This gives a principled way to decide when only partial or qualitative preference data are available.
  2. "The role of mixed discounting in risk-averse sequential decision-making" (with Erick Delage and Shanshan Wang). Studies the tension between risk aversion and time consistency in multistage decision problems. We develop an axiomatic framework for mixed discounting and show that it admits dynamically consistent risk-averse policies, together with a robustness analysis under CVaR-type criteria. The framework reconciles behavioral discounting with risk-aware evaluation in a single model.
  3. "Stochastic approximation for risk-aware Markov decision processes" (with William B. Haskell). Provides a convergence-rate analysis of stochastic approximation for risk-aware Markov decision processes with general risk measures such as CVaR. The analysis extends classical SA theory to objectives that are non-smooth and non-linear in the transition kernel. This paper laid the foundation for the risk-aware reinforcement learning line of my group.
  4. "Optimal partial centralization under decentralized self-selection and processing bottlenecks" (with Xiaobo Li, Yinuo Lin and Lei Xu). Considers a decentralized processing system in which facilities self-select into a shared platform, creating endogenous congestion at processing bottlenecks. We characterize the optimal degree of centralization and show that partial, rather than full, centralization is optimal under realistic self-selection behavior. The model offers concrete guidance for platform and capacity design in logistics and service operations.
  5. "Robust data-driven quasi-concave optimization" (with Jian Wu, William B. Haskell and Huifu Xu). Many practically important objectives, such as probabilities, utilities and ratios, are quasi-concave but not concave, and available data on them is limited. We propose a robust framework that optimizes the worst-case value of a quasi-concave objective over a data-driven ambiguity set, with tractable reformulations backed by computational tools. The framework yields reliable decisions from limited data where plug-in approaches can be badly misleading.
  6. "Robust assortment optimization under nested logit model" (with Yicheng Liu and Zizhuo Wang). Gives the first exact solutions to robust assortment optimization under the nested logit model when choice parameters are estimated with uncertainty. The worst-case structure turns out to differ fundamentally from the multinomial logit case, requiring new arguments. The resulting assortment policies come with explicit optimality guarantees.
  7. "A unified minimax framework for online non-convex long-term constrained optimization" (with Shijie Pan and Jianyu Xu). Online learning with long-term constraints and non-convex losses arises in scheduling, allocation and fairness-aware systems. We propose a unified primal-dual minimax framework that controls both regret and constraint violation under a single analysis, unifying previously scattered problem classes. The framework subsumes and sharpens several ad hoc algorithms in the literature.
  8. "Data-and-structure-driven transshipment policy in heterogeneous multi-retailer system" (with Yaping Zhao, Yimo Yan, Weilei Feng, Linhui Fu, Yong-Hong Kuo and Zhen Li). Lateral transshipment has largely been studied in homogeneous systems with two locations. We develop a structure-parameterized reinforcement learning approach for heterogeneous multi-retailer networks, embedding known structural properties of optimal policies directly into the training objective. The resulting policies generalize classical results to arbitrary network sizes and outperform both classical heuristics and vanilla deep RL in simulation.
  9. "DRL-ORA: Distributional reinforcement learning with online epistemic risk adaptation" (with Yupeng Wu, Wenyun Li and Chin Pang Ho). Distributional reinforcement learning captures the full return distribution but typically fixes the risk measure before any data is seen. DRL-ORA adapts the epistemic risk level online as evidence accumulates, balancing distributional uncertainty against risk-awareness during training. The resulting policies are robust early on yet sharpen as learning proceeds.
  10. "Target-based semi-amortized dynamic programming for complex crowd evacuation" (with Shijie Pan, Xudong Wu, Qixiu Cheng and Jiayu Chen). Evacuation planning in complex venues requires solving dynamic programs with enormous state spaces and risk-sensitive objectives. We propose a target-based semi-amortized dynamic programming method that amortizes expensive subproblems while preserving target-based risk evaluation. The method scales to realistic venue layouts and supports concrete crowd-management decisions.
