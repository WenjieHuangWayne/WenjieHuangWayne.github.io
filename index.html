Short Bio

I was born and grew up in Shanghai, China. I received the B.S. degree in Industrial Engineering and Management from Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU), Jun 2014, with a minor in Economics. I spent a wonderful Summer 2012 at the Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge, UK, as an exchange student. I also had internship experience in the manufacturing industry and in a public policy consulting firm.

I was fortunate to receive the Ph.D. degree from the Department of Industrial Systems Engineering and Management, National University of Singapore (NUS), Jun 2019, supervised by Prof. William B. Haskell (now at the Supply Chain and Operations Management Department, Mitchell E. Daniels, Jr. School of Business, Purdue University) and Prof. Tang Loon Ching. During my doctoral study, I also had the opportunity to collaborate with Prof. Huifu Xu, and participated in the E2S2-CREATE project under the Campus for Research Excellence and Technological Enterprise, Singapore.

Prior to the current post, recruited by the HKU-100 Global Recruitment Exercise, I held joint postdoc positions at the School of Data Science, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, China, and the Department of Decision Sciences, HEC Montréal, Québec, Canada (also affiliated with the Shenzhen Research Institute of Big Data (SRIBD) and the Group for Research in Decision Analysis (GERAD)), from Sep 2019 to Sep 2021, working with Prof. Erick Delage and Prof. Zizhuo Wang.

Selected Works

  1. "Preference robust optimization for choice functions on the space of CDFs" (with William B. Haskell and Huifu Xu). Moves preference robust optimization beyond expected utility by optimizing over choice functions defined on the space of CDFs, a class that contains both von Neumann-Morgenstern expected utility and Yaari's dual theory as special cases. Ambiguity is handled by a maximin model over choice functions elicited from available preference information, evaluated through an explicit characterization of the quasiconcave envelope of Lipschitz functions. Two numerical methods, a projected level function method and a level search method, solve the resulting problem and are demonstrated on portfolio optimization.
  2. "The role of mixed discounting in risk-averse sequential decision-making" (with Erick Delage and Shanshan Wang). Proposes a principled model for risk-preference mapping in infinite-horizon cash flows, combining conventional discounting of future income with a random interruption time, so that value and risk are discounted together. Field-experiment data support the model over the traditional one for 30% of participants, and for more than 80% once reasonable discount factors are enforced. The paper also characterizes when infinite-horizon risk stays finite, and when mixed discounting coincides with, or bounds, the risk perceived under the traditional approach.
  3. "Stochastic approximation for risk-aware Markov decision processes" (with William B. Haskell). Establishes almost-sure convergence and an explicit convergence rate for stochastic approximation in risk-aware Markov decision processes, with an inner loop solving a stochastic saddle-point problem for the risk and an outer loop performing Q-learning. The guarantees cover conditional value-at-risk, optimized certainty equivalent and absolute semideviation under a single analysis. The result was selected as a featured publication by the IEEE CSS-DES Newsletter.
  4. "Optimal partial centralization under decentralized self-selection and processing bottlenecks" (with Xiaobo Li, Yinuo Lin and Lei Xu). Proposes a partial centralization model in which a planner chooses how much of a fixed resource pool to centralize, trading better supply-demand information against the processing bottlenecks that centralization creates. A two-stage stochastic program with a two-piece linear efficiency function yields monotonicity-based structural properties and an algorithm that scales nearly linearly in the number of demand-class-scenario pairs, identifying all optimal centralization levels. The analysis delivers a counter-intuitive finding, namely that higher centralized efficiency need not justify more centralization, and a humanitarian-aid case study shows substantial shortage-cost reductions from partial decentralization.
  5. "Robust data-driven quasi-concave optimization" (with Jian Wu, William B. Haskell and Huifu Xu). Optimizes a quasiconcave objective known only through a finite data sample, over an ambiguity set of quasiconcave functions that majorize the data. Instead of settling for the asymptotic subgradient or support-function methods used for this problem class, it constructs the worst-case upper level sets explicitly and solves the robust problem exactly by binary search over a logarithmic number of convex feasibility problems. This yields finite convergence to the global optimum with an explicit rate in the number of convex programs solved.
  6. "Robust assortment optimization under nested logit model" (with Yicheng Liu and Zizhuo Wang). Extends robust assortment optimization from the multinomial logit model to the nested logit model, where nest-level utilities and dissimilarity factors must both be estimated and standard multinomial-logit arguments no longer apply. Establishes a minimax equality showing that the robust value equals the deterministic problem evaluated at the lower-bound parameters, with an optimal nested-by-revenue assortment forming a saddle point, hence a tractable linear programming reformulation. Comparative statics show that the optimal assortment size grows monotonically with utility uncertainty yet moves non-monotonically with uncertainty in the dissimilarity factors.
  7. "A unified minimax framework for online non-convex long-term constrained optimization" (with Shijie Pan and Jianyu Xu). Studies online optimization in which target and constraint functions are adversarially generated and not necessarily convex, a setting where no sublinear-regret algorithm was previously available. Proposes a Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader type primal-dual algorithm, handling non-convexity through exponentially distributed perturbations in the primal and strongly concave logarithmic regularization in the dual, and achieves the first sublinear cumulative regret for this class of problems. The guarantee is validated on a long-term extreme-value constrained river pollution source identification problem.
  8. "Data-and-structure-driven transshipment policy in heterogeneous multi-retailer system" (with Yaping Zhao, Yimo Yan, Weilei Feng, Linhui Fu, Yong-Hong Kuo and Zhen Li). Formulates lateral transshipment in heterogeneous multi-retailer networks as an event-driven Markov decision process under independent non-homogeneous Poisson demands, a setting where the classical two-retailer analysis does not carry over. The analysis pins down a fixed target replenishment structure, derives two marginal value bounds, and shows that a monotone inventory effect produces an explicit transshipment threshold in single-supplier states; for larger networks, a structure-parameterized PPO embeds these bounds as soft penalties in the critic. On a calibrated case the resulting policy cuts mean cost by 3.78% relative to a greedy benchmark and wins in 17 of 20 emergency-cost and capacity settings.
  9. "DRL-ORA: Distributional reinforcement learning with online epistemic risk adaptation" (with Yupeng Wu, Wenyun Li and Chin Pang Ho). Quantifies epistemic and implicit aleatory uncertainty within a single distributional reinforcement learning framework, and adjusts the epistemic risk level online by solving a total-variation minimization with a Follow-the-Leader type algorithm. The construction generalizes existing risk-adaptation schemes while making the choice of risk level explainable rather than hand-tuned. Across several task classes the adaptive policy outperforms methods that rely on fixed risk levels or manually designed risk schedules.
  10. "Target-based semi-amortized dynamic programming for complex crowd evacuation" (with Shijie Pan, Xudong Wu, Qixiu Cheng and Jiayu Chen). Anchors evacuation safety on corridor thresholds taken from width, regulation or drills, yielding a target-based safety measure whose level is inferred from data rather than tuned, so no risk-aversion parameter is required. Routes combine corridor risk by the worst corridor and trade it against walking distance, re-planning only at decision points, with a bound on how estimation error propagates across stages and an axiomatization of the resulting rules. A digital-twin study of the Louvre reports the lowest threshold-violation rate across nearly all settings, with performance almost insensitive to tuning.
